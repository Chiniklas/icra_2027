\subsection{Problem Formulation}

We decompose cyclic nut threading into the sequential execution of the ordered
subtask set
\begin{equation}
  \begin{aligned}
  \mathcal S=(
  &\mathrm{approach},\mathrm{grasp},\mathrm{rotate},\\
  &\mathrm{goal\ reaching},\mathrm{release},\mathrm{return}),
  \end{aligned}
  \label{eq:cycle_subtasks}
\end{equation}
The RL-learned expert policy executes approach, grasp, rotation, and goal
reaching, whereas release and return are implemented as empirically designed,
hard-coded motions.

Let $K$ be the predefined cycle count, $\Delta\varphi^*$ the desired clockwise
rotation per cycle, and $\varphi_k$ the unwrapped nut angle upon completing
goal reaching in cycle $k$. We define the cycle and episodic tracking errors as
\begin{equation}
  \begin{aligned}
  e_k&=\varphi_k-\varphi_{k-1}-\Delta\varphi^*,\\
  e_{\mathrm{ep}}&=\varphi_K-\varphi_0-K\Delta\varphi^*
                    =\sum_{k=1}^{K}e_k,\\
  \mathcal E(\tau)&=\frac{1}{K}\sum_{k=1}^{K}e_k^2
                     +\beta e_{\mathrm{ep}}^2.
  \end{aligned}
  \label{eq:tracking_errors}
\end{equation}
Positive (negative) errors indicate overshoot (lag); $\mathcal E$ squares them
to prevent cancellation.

Our research problem is then to find the policy that chains all subtasks with
maximum completion success and minimum tracking error over the episode. Let
$\Pi_{\mathcal S}$ denote the policies that execute $\mathcal S$ in order in
every cycle. We express the two priorities as
\begin{equation}
  \begin{aligned}
  \Pi_{\mathrm{succ}}
    &=\arg\max_{\pi\in\Pi_{\mathcal S}}
      \Pr_{\tau\sim p_\pi}\!\left[C(\tau)=K\right],\\
  \pi^*
    &=\arg\min_{\pi\in\Pi_{\mathrm{succ}}}
      \mathbb E_{\tau\sim p_\pi}\!\left[
        \mathcal E(\tau)\mid C(\tau)=K\right].
  \end{aligned}
  \label{eq:episode_objective}
\end{equation}
Here, $C(\tau)$ counts completed cycles. Equation~\eqref{eq:episode_objective}
first maximizes $K$-cycle success and then minimizes tracking cost, with
$\beta$ weighting accumulated drift relative to cycle-wise accuracy. We set
$K=10$ and $\Delta\varphi^*=60^\circ$, and learn $\pi^*$ from fixed
hybrid-teacher demonstrations rather than through on-hardware optimization.

\subsection{Overview of the Distillation Pipeline}

\begin{figure*}[t]
  \centering
  \includegraphics[width=0.90\textwidth]{mainpipeline.pdf}
  \caption{Detailed distillation training pipeline. A simulated dataset is
    collected and replayed in the real world, where human corrections augment
    the dataset. Each RGB-D snapshot is processed by the DP3 vision encoder
    into a 64-D visual feature, which is concatenated with a 256-D state and
    task encoding. The combined representation conditions a conditional
    flow-matching model that predicts an 8-step action chunk.}
  \label{fig:main_pipeline}
\end{figure*}

The proposed pipeline, illustrated in \figref{fig:main_pipeline}, comprises
three stages. First, we train a privileged
turning policy in simulation and combine it with scripted release, return, and
re-engagement motions to construct a hybrid teacher. Second, we use the hybrid
teacher to generate complete cyclic demonstrations in simulation and replay
the resulting trajectories on the real system. A human operator intervenes
whenever a grasp failure is anticipated. We consolidate the simulated and
human-corrected real-world demonstrations into a fixed offline dataset. Third,
we encode the student's depth and proprioceptive observations and use
conditional flow matching to distill the demonstrated action-chunk
distribution into a single visuomotor policy.

\subsection{Privileged Teacher Policy Training}

\subsubsection{Simplified Thread Simulation}
\begin{figure}[t]
  \centering
  \includegraphics[width=0.82\columnwidth]{simplified_thread_simulation-v3.png}
  \caption{Coupled revolute--prismatic thread model, with
    $k=-\rho/(2\pi)$ in \eqref{eq:revolute_prismatic_coupling}.}
  \label{fig:simplified_thread_simulation}
\end{figure}
Our simplified thread model couples a passive revolute joint to a coaxial,
position-controlled prismatic joint, avoiding explicit thread contacts, as
illustrated in \figref{fig:simplified_thread_simulation}. With prismatic
coordinate $d_t$, unwrapped direction-corrected revolute coordinate $\theta_t$,
thread pitch $\rho$, and turn-start index $b_k$, the prismatic target is
\begin{equation}
  d_t^* - d_{b_k}
  = -\frac{\rho}{2\pi}\left(\theta_t-\theta_{b_k}\right),
  \label{eq:revolute_prismatic_coupling}
\end{equation}
so one revolution produces one thread pitch of axial travel. Finger--nut
contact forces remain active, while the revolute--prismatic mechanism
constrains lateral motion and keeps the nut near the commanded helix. The
finite axial-drive stiffness permits small deviations from this path. For
revolute-joint velocity $\omega_t$, smoothed Coulomb and viscous friction give
$\bar{\tau}_{f,t}=-\tau_c\tanh(\omega_t/\omega_s)-c_v\omega_t$, where $\tau_c$
is the Coulomb friction torque, $c_v$ is the viscous damping coefficient, and
$\omega_s$ is the smoothing speed.

\subsubsection{PPO Training}
We train the privileged teacher using an asymmetric recurrent actor--critic.
The actor input contains noisy fingertip pose, fingertip velocity,
nut-relative grasp state, turning-target error, a grasp-phase flag, the previous
action, and a nut-size one-hot code. The critic follows the same task and
history structure using clean simulator values, and extends it with arm-joint
positions, complete nut--bolt geometry, controller state, and wrist and
fingertip contact state. The one-hot code conditions a single teacher on M24,
M30, and M36 hexagonal nuts. The actor and
central critic each use a two-layer LSTM with 1024 hidden units per layer,
followed by a $512$--$128$--$64$ ELU MLP; recurrent sequences span 128 control
steps. The actor parameterizes a diagonal Gaussian over the normalized action
$\mathbf{a}_t\in[-1,1]^9$.

We use a 9-D action space comprising a 6-D Cartesian controller target native
to the Franka arm and three finger-control dimensions.

The resulting recurrent policy
$\pi_T(\mathbf{a}\mid\mathbf{z}^{\pi},\mathbf{h})$ is optimized with PPO using
the shaped reward
\begin{equation}
  \begin{aligned}
  r_t &= \sum_{i\in\mathcal{R}} w_i^rR_{i,t}
         -\sum_{j\in\mathcal{P}} w_j^pP_{j,t},\\
  \mathcal{R} &= \{\mathrm{center,face,grasp,turn,target}\},\\
  \mathcal{P} &= \{\mathrm{mag},\Delta a,\mathrm{align},\mathrm{force},
  \mathrm{settle},\mathrm{clear},\mathrm{contact}\}.
  \end{aligned}
  \label{eq:teacher_reward}
\end{equation}
The reward follows three stages. During approach,
$R_t^{\mathrm{center}}$ rewards motion toward the nut center and
$R_t^{\mathrm{face}}$ rewards alignment with the designated opposing faces.
$R_t^{\mathrm{grasp}}$ marks acquisition of a stable proper grasp. During
turning, $R_t^{\mathrm{turn}}$ rewards grip-maintaining progress toward the
fixed helical target. At completion, $R_t^{\mathrm{target}}$ rewards holding
the target stably. The approach and turning rewards pay only new-best progress,
preventing repeated motion from accumulating reward.

The penalties discourage large commands ($P_t^{\mathrm{mag}}$), abrupt action
changes ($P_t^{\Delta a}$), hand--nut misalignment ($P_t^{\mathrm{align}}$),
excess wrist force ($P_t^{\mathrm{force}}$), residual rotation near the target
($P_t^{\mathrm{settle}}$), insufficient table clearance
($P_t^{\mathrm{clear}}$), and arm--table contact ($P_t^{\mathrm{contact}}$).

\subsection{Hybrid-Teacher Dataset Construction with Real-World Fine-Tuning}

\subsubsection{Cyclic Teacher Rollout in Simulation}
In simulation, we combine the learned turning policy with scripted release,
collision-free return, and re-engagement motions. Let
$m_t\in\mathcal{M}_E=\{T,W_1,\ldots,W_J,R\}$ denote the turning phase, release
and return waypoints, and re-engagement. The hybrid teacher is
\begin{equation}
  \pi_E(\mathbf{x}_t,m_t)=
  \begin{cases}
    \bar\pi_T(\mathbf{z}^{\pi}_t,\mathbf{h}_t), & m_t=T,\\
    \boldsymbol\kappa_{m_t}(\mathbf{x}_t), & m_t\neq T,
  \end{cases}
  \label{eq:hybrid_expert}
\end{equation}
where $\boldsymbol\kappa_{m_t}$ is the scripted controller for the active
transition phase; both branches use the same filtered, bolt-relative Cartesian
controller action space. Release is triggered when the rotation lies within $\pm5^\circ$ of
$\varphi_{\mathrm{tar}}$ ($60^\circ$ in our experiments), while a valid
opposed-finger grasp is maintained for a short dwell period. The fingers then
open to a safe clearance, after which scripted arm--hand waypoints return to the
initial grasp pose and re-engage the nut. The turning reference is updated at
re-engagement, but the physical nut is not reset, producing complete
multi-cycle demonstrations.

\subsubsection{Data Augmentation under Human Intervention}
We replay simulated trajectories on the real system through the same Cartesian
controller. When slip or misalignment is anticipated, an operator replaces the
replayed teacher actions until a stable grasp is recovered. These human actions
overwrite the corresponding teacher actions in the trajectory, and the
resulting corrected trajectory is recorded as a real-world demonstration.

Before training, we combine simulated rollouts and human-corrected replays into
a fixed offline dataset of depth observations, proprioception, normalized task
progress, phase encodings, and executed commands. The student never acts during
collection. We mask chunks that cross physical resets, retain phase boundaries,
and pad terminal chunks with the final valid command.

\subsection{Student Policy Distillation}
\label{sec:flow_distillation}

Student learning is posed as supervised imitation from the fixed dataset of
simulated hybrid-teacher and human-corrected demonstrations,
$\mathcal D=\mathcal D_{\mathrm{sim}}\cup\mathcal D_{\mathrm{real}}$. The
student never acts during data collection. At time $t$, it observes
$\mathbf{y}_t=(\mathbf{D}_t,\mathbf{s}_t,p_t,\mathbf{e}_{m_t})$
and learns the distribution of the executed $H$-step action chunk.

Following DP3~\cite{ze2024dp3}, we discard RGB and back-project depth into a
cropped, normalized point cloud $\mathcal{P}_t$. A compact PointNet with
$64$--$128$--$256$ pointwise layers and global max pooling encodes
$\mathcal{P}_t$ into a 64-D feature $\mathbf{v}_t$, which is fused with
proprioception, task progress, and phase information. A $128$--$128$ ELU MLP
encodes proprioception, and fusion yields the 256-D condition $\mathbf{c}_t$.
With $H=8$, a four-layer non-causal Transformer (512-D width, eight heads, and
a 2048-D GELU MLP) predicts velocities for the eight 9-D action tokens,
conditioned on $\mathbf{c}_t$ and a sinusoidal flow-time embedding.
During distillation, we follow DEXTRAH-RGB-style lighting and texture
randomization to improve robustness and reduce the sim-to-real
gap~\cite{dextrahrgb}.

The supervision target is the executed action chunk
$\mathbf{U}_t^D=[\mathbf{u}^D_t,\ldots,\mathbf{u}^D_{t+H-1}]
\in\mathbb{R}^{H\times9}$. We formulate its distillation as conditional flow
matching:
\begin{equation}
  \begin{aligned}
  \theta^*=\arg\min_\theta\quad&
  \mathbb E\!\left[
    \left\|v_\theta(\mathbf U_t^\lambda,\lambda\mid\mathbf c_t)
    -\dot{\mathbf U}_t^\lambda\right\|_2^2
  \right],\\
  \text{s.t.}\quad
  &\mathbf U_t^\lambda
    =(1-\lambda)\mathbf U_t^0+\lambda\mathbf U_t^D,\\
  &\dot{\mathbf U}_t^\lambda=\mathbf U_t^D-\mathbf U_t^0.
  \end{aligned}
  \label{eq:flow_objective}
\end{equation}
The expectation is over
$(\mathbf y_t,\mathbf U_t^D)\sim\mathcal D$,
$\mathbf U_t^0\sim\mathcal N(\mathbf0,\mathbf I)$, and
$\lambda\sim\mathcal U[0,1]$. The condition $\mathbf y_t$ contains depth,
proprioception, task progress, and phase information and is encoded as
$\mathbf c_t$. The learned velocity field transports Gaussian noise to the
conditional action distribution $p_\theta(\mathbf U_t\mid\mathbf y_t)$.
