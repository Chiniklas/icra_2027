\begin{abstract}
Learning to perform controlled nut threading is one of the most challenging
problems in robotic assembly. A policy must transition seamlessly among
repetitive phases---including grasping, turning, goal reaching, releasing,
returning, and resetting---to sustain consecutive threading cycles, making
the task long-horizon and sensitive to policy-chaining errors. We present
\methodname, a hierarchical
policy-distillation framework for learning stable and accurate cyclic nut
threading across multiple nut sizes. Rather than training a monolithic policy
for the complete task, a privileged reinforcement-learning teacher first
learns a goal-conditioned turning skill in simulation. We chain this skill
with scripted transition behaviors to generate complete multi-cycle
demonstrations. During real-world replays, human interventions selectively
correct impending failures and augment the demonstrations.
The resulting mixture of simulated and real-world demonstrations is used to
distill a student policy through conditional flow matching (CFM). A
DP3-inspired encoder converts RGB-D observations into point-cloud features
used to predict eight-step action chunks. We evaluate the student policy in
10-cycle trials using in-distribution M24, M30, and M36 hexagonal nuts and
unseen rectangular, octagonal, and round nut geometries. For all
in-distribution nuts, the policy completes all 10 cycles, with mean cyclic
tracking errors below $6^\circ$ in magnitude. The policy also generalizes
successfully to round and octagonal nuts with comparable error levels, but
fails to generalize to rectangular nuts.
\end{abstract}
