\subsection{Conclusion}

In this paper, we introduced a hybrid-expert construction that combines an RL-learned
single-turn threading policy with scripted transition motions. This design
mitigates policy-chaining errors that can destabilize long-horizon execution,
enabling reliable cyclic demonstrations.

Human intervention further contributes to dataset
construction by shifting the demonstrated action distribution toward
real-world-executable behaviors, substantially reducing the sim-to-real gap.

Finally, pairing a DP3-style visual encoder with a conditional flow-matching
action backbone captures both repetitive motion structure and subtle
cycle-to-cycle initial-state drift. This combination enables sample-efficient
imitation learning for long-horizon tasks spanning more than
\SI{200}{\second} of wall-clock execution.

\subsection{Future Work}

Long-horizon stress tests reveal a clear limitation: the current student
policy eventually fails after 15--22 cycles, depending on nut size, geometry,
and test conditions. This degradation appears largely due to limited domain
randomization along the $z$-axis, which inadequately covers the axial drift
accumulated over many cycles. An important next step is therefore to introduce
a curriculum that progressively expands the axial variation encountered
during training, improving the long-horizon capability of the RL teacher and,
consequently, the distilled student.

To simplify the task, we deliberately initialize the nut already engaged with
the bolt. We adopted this setting after unsuccessful attempts to learn or
demonstrate nut engagement using reinforcement learning, teleoperation, and
imitation learning. We attribute much of the difficulty to the rigid nut and
the helical thread constraint, which admit only a narrow entry configuration
for successful engagement. Future work will therefore focus on autonomous nut
engagement, which we anticipate will require fine-grained fingertip-force
feedback and tactile sensing.

Moreover, the current pipeline is limited to
common hexagonal and similarly compact, rounded nut profiles. Specialized
geometries such as butterfly (wing) nuts, which may require different grasp
and regrasp strategies, are not considered; extending the method to such nut
families is another direction for future work.
