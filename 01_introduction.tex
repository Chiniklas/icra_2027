Threading a nut onto a bolt is among the most difficult tasks in robotic
assembly because threaded engagement is notoriously contact-rich. On the one
hand, the engaged threads can produce more than 1,000 simultaneous contact
points, making faithful simulation prohibitively expensive for the large number
of parallel rollouts required for reinforcement learning. Factory
directly addresses this computational bottleneck through a combination of
signed-distance-field (SDF) collision detection, contact reduction, and a
Gauss--Seidel solver, enabling 1,000 parallel nut--bolt assemblies to be
simulated in real time on a single GPU~\cite{narang2022factory}.

On the other hand, depending on the task, humans perform nut threading in
various ways. For simple spinning, a single finger is sufficient while the
wrist remains fixed. A pinch grasp is preferred for controlled threading,
whereas a tripod or even a power grasp may be applied in high-resistance
scenarios.

Prior work has typically approached the problem from the perspective of
spinning. DexScrew uses a dexterous hand--arm setup similar to ours and replaces
explicit thread geometry with a revolute-joint proxy to learn a rotational
finger gait. The learned skill then assists teleoperation and supports the
collection of real-world demonstrations for tactile behavior
cloning~\cite{dexscrew}. However, its policy performs uncontrolled spinning
without target conditioning or learned termination, leaving goal-directed,
cyclic nut threading unresolved.

Play2Perfect addresses controlled screwing through a specialized task
formulation. It first learns a goal-conditioned dexterous manipulation prior
on a rod-shaped object and then fine-tunes the prior on a furniture leg that is
three times as long, using CAD-derived intermediate pose
goals~\cite{play2perfect}. This formulation enables goal-directed screwing in a
structured setting but does not address everyday threaded objects, such as nuts
and bolts.

We first attempted to approach the controlled threading problem for nuts and bolts
by training a unified teacher policy for the complete
multi-cycle task. In practice, however, designing rewards that capture both
long-horizon progress and precise transitions between phases proved exceedingly
difficult.

Therefore, we formulate this problem as a long-horizon
skill-chaining problem. The approach, grasp, rotate, goal-reaching, release, and return phases
must be executed sequentially with precise handoffs. Our approach adopts a
three-level hierarchy. At the lowest level, a learned teacher executes the
elemental turning motion. At the middle level, scripted transition policies
chain these turns into complete simulated trajectories, which are corrected
through human intervention during real-world replay. At the highest level, a
student policy distills these demonstrations into a single deployable
visuomotor policy. We evaluate threading accuracy in both simulation and
real-world experiments.

The main contributions of this work are:
\begin{itemize}
  \item A hierarchical learning framework for long-horizon cyclic dexterous
    manipulation that decomposes nut threading into a learned goal-conditioned
    turning skill and structured transition behaviors, avoiding the difficulty
    of training a monolithic policy over repeated contact-rich manipulation
    cycles.

  \item A hybrid-teacher demonstration framework that chains an RL-trained
    dexterous manipulation policy with scripted release, return, and
    re-engagement behaviors, enabling scalable generation of coherent
    long-horizon demonstrations across repeated manipulation cycles.

  \item A human-corrected sim-to-real data-augmentation strategy that
    selectively replaces failure-prone segments during real-world replay with
    corrective demonstrations, efficiently adapting simulated long-horizon
    behaviors to real-world contact and perception discrepancies.

  \item A unified visuomotor policy distilled from heterogeneous long-horizon
    demonstrations, combining 3D point-cloud observations with conditional
    flow matching to execute all manipulation phases without privileged state,
    scripted transitions, or human intervention at deployment.
\end{itemize}
