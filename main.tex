\begin{abstract}
Long-horizon cyclic nut threading combines contact-rich turning with reliable
release, return, and re-engagement transitions. We found that training a
single reinforcement-learning policy end to end across all of these phases
makes long-horizon reward design and stable phase transitions difficult.
We therefore present \methodname, which follows a simple principle: decompose
during data generation, unify during deployment. A privileged RL policy learns
only goal-conditioned turning, while scripted controllers execute the
transition phases; together, they form a hybrid teacher that generates
complete multi-cycle demonstrations. During real-world replay, human
interventions correct impending failures caused by sim-to-real discrepancies
and augment the offline dataset. We then use conditional flow matching to
distill the simulated and human-corrected demonstrations into a single
visuomotor student. A DP3-inspired encoder converts RGB-D observations into
point-cloud features used to predict eight-step action chunks. At deployment,
the student executes the complete cyclic task without privileged state,
scripted controllers, or human intervention. We evaluate the student policy in
10-cycle trials using in-distribution M24, M30, and M36 hexagonal nuts and
unseen rectangular, octagonal, and round nut geometries. For all
in-distribution nuts, the policy completes all 10 cycles, with mean cyclic
tracking errors below $6^\circ$ in magnitude. The policy also generalizes
successfully to round and octagonal nuts with comparable error levels, but
fails to generalize to rectangular nuts.
\end{abstract}
