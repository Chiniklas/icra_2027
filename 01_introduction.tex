Threading a nut onto a bolt is among the most difficult tasks in robotic
assembly. Yet humans perform it with apparent ease by adapting their grasp to
the nut size and threading resistance: a loose nut can be spun with a single
finger, increasing reaction torque may prompt a switch to a pinch grasp, and a
large or tight nut may require a tripod or power grasp. This flexibility
requires continuous coordination of grasp selection, applied torque, and task
progress.

Existing methods make dexterous screwing tractable by adding substantial
structure to the problem. DexScrew replaces explicit thread geometry with a
revolute-joint proxy to learn a rotational finger gait, then uses the learned
skill to assist teleoperation and collect real-world demonstrations for tactile
behavior cloning~\cite{dexscrew}. However, the resulting policy achieves only
uncontrolled spinning: it sustains rotation but neither accepts a target
rotation or thread position nor determines when that target has been reached.
It therefore cannot terminate precisely, limiting its use in tasks such as
turning a valve to a prescribed setting.

Play2Perfect addresses controlled screwing through a different form of task
structure. It first learns a goal-conditioned dexterous manipulation prior
on a rod shaped object, then fine-tunes it on a three-times-longer furniture leg using
CAD-derived intermediate pose goals~\cite{play2perfect}. This formulation
enables goal-directed screwing in a structured setting, but leaves open
autonomous, long-horizon nut threading with realistic contact and coordinated
arm--hand control.

We study controlled nut--bolt threading under moderate resistance, where a
pinch grasp and coordinated arm--hand manipulation are required. The robot must
repeatedly approach, grasp, rotate, release, return, and re-engage, making the
task inherently cyclic and long-horizon. We evaluate threading accuracy in both
simulation and real-world experiments.

The main contributions of this work are:
\begin{itemize}
  \item A reinforcement-learning pipeline for training a teacher policy that
    operates across multiple nut sizes and rotates an already-engaged nut
    \(60^\circ\) clockwise.

  \item A hybrid teacher that combines the learned rotation policy with scripted
    transition motions to generate long-horizon demonstrations for student
    distillation. The teacher achieves perfect task completion in simulation;
    we also quantify its reduced success rate in the real world.

  \item A human-in-the-loop real-world data-collection procedure that replaces
    failed autonomous rollouts with human-guided corrections for fine-tuning.

  \item A visuomotor student-distillation pipeline that consolidates
    long-horizon hybrid-teacher demonstrations and human corrections into a
    single flow-matching policy with a DP3-inspired visual encoder.
\end{itemize}
