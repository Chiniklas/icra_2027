Threading is a fundamental operation in robotic assembly, maintenance, and
disassembly~\cite{siciliano2016springer}. Although humans can readily rotate a
nut while maintaining its engagement with a mating thread, autonomous
execution remains challenging.
Threading requires the robot to regulate motion through sustained contact under
tight geometric tolerances, uncertain friction, and only partially observed
interaction forces. Small pose errors can cause slipping, jamming, or
cross-threading, while excessive force can damage the workpiece. These coupled
perception, control, and contact constraints make controlled threading one of
the most difficult contact-rich manipulation tasks.

The difficulty becomes more pronounced over long horizons. Because the motion
range of a gripper or robot wrist is limited, completing a threading operation
requires a cyclic sequence of partial rotations, timely release, collision-free
return, and re-engagement. An error in any cycle changes the initial condition
of the next, so small deviations can accumulate until the task fails. Purely
scripted trajectories cannot react to these deviations, whereas a monolithic
learned policy must discover both fine contact behavior and long-horizon phase
transitions. A practical solution should therefore combine a reactive policy
for controlled threading with an explicit mechanism for reliably chaining the
policy across many cycles.

We introduce \methodname, a framework for long-horizon cyclic threading that
combines learned reactive control with structured policy chaining, as shown in
\figref{fig:teaser}. We first train teacher policies in a simplified simulation
that preserves the contact interactions needed for threading while reducing
irrelevant complexity. We then chain the learned behavior using a release-time
criterion and a state-machine-based scripted return motion designed to remain
collision-free. Finally, we distill the chained teacher behavior into a
vision-based student policy. The student uses a DP3 vision head to encode the
scene and is trained from collected teacher demonstrations using flow-matching
imitation learning.

The main contributions of this work are:
\begin{itemize}
  \item a controlled-threading framework that couples a reactive learned policy
    with explicit release timing and a collision-free return state machine for
    long-horizon cyclic execution;
  \item a teacher-to-student training pipeline that combines simplified
    simulation, a DP3 visual representation, and flow-matching imitation
    learning; and
  \item an evaluation across M24, M30, and M36 nuts with hexagonal, octagonal,
    rectangular, and round geometries, using maximum cycle count, threading
    tracking error, and success rate to assess robustness and generalization.
\end{itemize}
