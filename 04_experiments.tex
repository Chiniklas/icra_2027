\subsection{Evaluation Metrics}

Each condition is evaluated over $N=10$ trials with a nominal horizon of ten
cycles. One cycle comprises a $60^\circ$ clockwise turn followed by successful
release, return, and re-engagement.

\paragraph{Maximum Cycle Count}
For trial $i$, $C_i\in[0,10]$ is the number of consecutive complete cycles
before the first grasp loss, transition failure, safety termination, or
timeout. An incomplete final cycle is not counted. The tables report the mean
per-trial maximum, $\bar C=N^{-1}\sum_i C_i$; higher is better.

\paragraph{Cyclic Tracking Error}
Cyclic tracking error measures the angular accuracy of each completed turn.
Let $\Delta\theta_{i,k}$ be the clockwise nut rotation in cycle $k$ of trial
$i$. The per-cycle error is $e_{i,k}=\Delta\theta_{i,k}-60^\circ$. Across all
$M=\sum_i C_i$ completed cycles, we report the mean
$\mu_{\mathrm{cyc}}=M^{-1}\sum_{i,k}e_{i,k}$ and its standard deviation
$\sigma_{\mathrm{cyc}}$. Thus, these statistics describe tracking accuracy only
during successful cycles. Negative error denotes lagging and positive error
denotes overshooting; a mean closer to zero and a smaller standard deviation
indicate more accurate and consistent turning.

\paragraph{Ten-Cycle Tracking Error}
Ten-cycle tracking error compares the accumulated rotation from successful
cycles with the nominal $10\times60^\circ=600^\circ$ target. Let
$\Theta_i=\sum_{k=1}^{C_i}\Delta\theta_{i,k}$; rotation from an incomplete final
cycle is not counted. We define $e_{10,i}=\Theta_i-600^\circ$ and report its
mean over the 10 trials. Equivalently, the reported value is
$\mu_{10}=\bar C(60^\circ+\mu_{\mathrm{cyc}})-600^\circ$. If no cycle succeeds,
both tracking-error metrics are undefined and reported as ``--.'' All tracking
errors use the same sign convention and are reported in degrees.

\subsection{Experimental Setup}

We simulate a seven-DoF Franka Research~3 (FR3) arm equipped with an Inspire
Hand RH56DFX using Isaac Sim~5.1.0 and Isaac Lab~2.2.1. The 9-D action space
encodes a six-DoF OSC target and three index--thumb joint commands. At
\SI{15}{Hz},
the student converts a calibrated $320\times180$ depth image into 4096 cropped
points and fuses a 64-dimensional DP3 feature with proprioception, progress,
and phase; physics runs at \SI{120}{Hz}. For the retained M24 run, we train the
PPO teacher for 200 epochs in 512 environments. Using only 20 successful
ten-cycle demonstrations (19,486 frames) collected from 32 environments, we
increase trajectory diversity by applying 30 random arm impulses to each
demonstration. We train the flow-matching student for 300 epochs using
eight-step action chunks.

\subsection{Ablations}

To investigate the contribution of individual components in our framework, we
conduct ablation studies focusing on four key aspects: \textbf{(Q1)} whether a
unified multi-cycle student outperforms repeated execution of a single-cycle
policy, \textbf{(Q2)} the contribution of the DP3 point-cloud encoder,
\textbf{(Q3)} the effectiveness of flow matching relative to deterministic
behavior cloning with LSTM and Transformer backbones, and
\textbf{(Q4)} the benefit of augmenting simulation demonstrations with
human-corrected real-world trajectories during student distillation. Results
are averaged over 10 trials on the M24 hexagonal nut and reported in
\tabref{tab:m24_ablation}.

We compare six variants under matched training and evaluation budgets,
changing only the component specified by each ablation.

\noindent\textbf{(a) Single-Cycle Student.}
We train on real-world-augmented single-cycle demonstrations and invoke the
resulting policy ten times, resetting its temporal context after each cycle.

\noindent\textbf{(b) Ours + ResNet Visual Encoder.}
We replace the DP3 point-cloud encoder with a ResNet-18 encoder operating
directly on depth images while retaining the same policy backbone,
flow-matching objective, dataset, and training budget as ours.

\noindent\textbf{(c) DP3 Vision + LSTM Distillation.}
Using the same real-world-augmented dataset and eight-step chunks as ours, we
replace the flow-matching Transformer with an LSTM backbone trained using
deterministic supervised action regression.

\noindent\textbf{(d) DP3 Vision + Transformer Distillation.}
Using the same real-world-augmented dataset, eight-step chunks, and Transformer
backbone as ours, we replace the flow-matching objective with deterministic
supervised action regression.

\noindent\textbf{(e) Ours with Only Simulated Data.}
We train the multi-cycle student using only simulated hybrid-teacher
demonstrations, excluding all human-corrected real-world data.

\noindent\textbf{(f) Ours.}
We train one flow-matching student on complete multi-cycle hybrid-teacher
demonstrations augmented with human-corrected real-world data.

To address \textbf{Q1}, we compare the single-cycle student (a) with our unified
multi-cycle student (f). The unified student completes all 10 cycles, whereas the
single-cycle student completes only 5.2 cycles on average. Although their per-cycle
tracking errors are similar, the single-cycle student exhibits a common failure mode:
after a few cycles, it slips off the upper edge of the nut and fails to track the nut
as it descends, even for small pitch advances. This behavior indicates that although
the single-cycle student captures the basic threading skill, it cannot adapt to shifts
in initial conditions or mitigate compounding errors over a long horizon. In contrast,
the multi-cycle student learns from full, long-horizon demonstrations and exhibits
sufficient recovery from and compensation for accumulated errors, resulting in
substantially stronger full-task completion performance
(\tabref{tab:m24_ablation}).

For \textbf{Q2}, we compare ours with a ResNet-18 visual encoder (b) against
our full model with the DP3 point-cloud encoder (f). Both variants use the same
policy backbone, flow-matching objective, training data, and evaluation budget,
isolating the contribution of the visual representation
(\tabref{tab:m24_ablation}).

\textbf{Q3} is evaluated against deterministic behavior-cloning baselines with
both LSTM and Transformer backbones. All methods use the same
real-world-augmented data and eight-step action chunks. DP3 vision with LSTM
distillation (c) completes no successful cycle, whereas our flow-matching
student (f) completes
all 10. DP3 vision with Transformer distillation (d) provides a controlled
comparison that isolates the effect of flow matching from that of the
Transformer backbone
(\tabref{tab:m24_ablation}).

For \textbf{Q4}, we contrast our simulation-only student (e) with our
real-world-augmented student (f). Adding human-corrected trajectories raises the
average maximum cycle count from 2.1 to 10, reduces the successful-cycle lag
from $-10.07^\circ$ to $-4.21^\circ$, and reduces the ten-cycle lag from
$-495.15^\circ$ to $-42.10^\circ$. The real-world data therefore improve both
sustained execution and angular tracking after transfer
(\tabref{tab:m24_ablation}).
