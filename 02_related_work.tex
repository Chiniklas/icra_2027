\textbf{Robot Assembly with Dexterous Manipulation.}
Robot assembly requires precise motion and closed-loop contact under geometric
and dynamic uncertainty~\cite{siciliano2016springer}, with tasks including
peg-in-hole insertion, gear meshing, connector mating, screwdriving, and
nut--bolt fastening. Classical systems rely on fixtures or specialized tools,
whereas multi-fingered hands offer greater versatility but introduce
high-dimensional control and complex contact dynamics. Learning-based systems
have advanced assembly through scalable simulation~\cite{narang2022factory},
force-guided contact search~\cite{noseworthy2025forge}, dexterous
tool use~\cite{simtoolreal}, and reusable manipulation priors~\cite{play2perfect}.
Closest to our work, DexScrew learns sustained rotation of an already engaged
fastener from simplified simulation~\cite{dexscrew}. In contrast,
\methodname{} performs controlled, multi-cycle nut threading with coordinated
turning, release, return, and re-engagement across multiple nut sizes.

\textbf{Policy Chaining in Long-Horizon Tasks.}
Training a single policy for a long-horizon task is challenging: small control
errors can compound over time~\cite{ross2011reduction}, while sequential
fine-tuning on new subtasks can overwrite previously acquired behaviors and
cause catastrophic forgetting~\cite{kirkpatrick2017overcoming}. Common remedies
include increasing network capacity, freezing or regularizing task-relevant
parameters, and adopting architectures with stronger temporal memory, such as
recurrent networks or Transformers. An alternative is to decompose the task
into reusable skills and chain their policies. This decomposition reduces the
burden on each individual policy but introduces a transition problem: the
terminal states produced by one skill may fall outside the initial-state
distribution of the next, causing failures at skill
boundaries~\cite{lee2022adversarial}. Sequential Dexterity addresses this mismatch using
a learned transition-feasibility function that fine-tunes adjacent dexterous
policies, supports autonomous switching, and enables
recovery~\cite{chen2023sequential}.

\textbf{Sim-to-Real Adaptation with Human Intervention.}
The sim-to-real gap is a long-standing problem in robot learning: policies
trained in simulation encounter mismatches in perception, dynamics, contact,
and control when deployed on physical hardware. These discrepancies become
increasingly consequential for high-dimensional, contact-rich dexterous
manipulation. Most approaches seek robust zero-shot transfer through domain
randomization~\cite{tobin2017domain} or domain
adaptation~\cite{wilcox2025adapt3r}, while others use limited real-world data
to fine-tune or augment a simulation-trained policy~\cite{jiang2025transic}.
DAgger aggregates expert labels on states visited by the learner, whereas
SafeDAgger reduces expert queries by requesting supervision only when the
learned policy is predicted to be unsafe~\cite{ross2011reduction,
zhang2016safedagger}. Similarly, during real-world hybrid-teacher replay, we
replace anticipated slip or misalignment failures with human corrections and
add the corrected trajectories to the student-distillation dataset.

\textbf{Visuomotor Policy Distillation.}
Teacher--student distillation provides a practical route from policies trained
with privileged simulator state to deployable policies that act from onboard
sensing~\cite{chen2020cheating}. In dexterous manipulation, DextrAH-RGB
distills a privileged reinforcement-learning teacher into an end-to-end visual
grasping policy~\cite{dextrahrgb}, while UniGraspTransformer consolidates
object-specific teacher policies into a unified student~\cite{wang2025unigrasp}.
For long-horizon tasks, predicting action sequences improves temporal
consistency, while generative models can represent multiple valid behaviors.
Diffusion Policy models multimodal action sequences through iterative
denoising~\cite{chi2023diffusion}. Flow matching instead learns a continuous
vector field for efficient ODE-based action
generation~\cite{lipman2023flow,black2024pi0}. These properties motivate its use for
distilling our hybrid-teacher and human-corrected demonstrations.
