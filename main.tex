\begin{abstract}
Autonomous nut threading requires a robot to sustain contact-rich manipulation
over repeated grasp, turn, release, and re-engagement cycles while remaining
robust to sim-to-real discrepancies. We present \methodname, a hierarchical
framework for learning this long-horizon behavior without training a single
reinforcement-learning policy for the entire task. A privileged recurrent
teacher first learns a goal-directed turning skill in a computationally
efficient thread simulation that couples rotational and prismatic motion. We
then combine the learned skill with scripted release and return motions to
generate complete cyclic demonstrations. These trajectories are replayed on
real hardware, where human interventions correct impending grasp failures,
producing a fixed dataset of simulated and real-world demonstrations. Finally,
we distill the dataset into one depth-conditioned visuomotor policy through
conditional flow matching. The student uses a compact point-cloud encoder and
a non-causal Transformer to predict eight-step chunks of unified Cartesian and
finger commands. At deployment, the student controls every task phase without
privileged state, scripted transitions, or human input. We evaluate the method
across multiple nut sizes and held-out nut geometries in simulation and on a
physical robot.
\end{abstract}
